Why Vector Databases Matter

By Jordan Example · March 3, 2026 · 6 min read

Search used to mean matching keywords. Modern applications instead compare meaning, which requires turning text, images and audio into embeddings and finding the closest ones quickly.

A vector database stores those embeddings and answers nearest-neighbour queries. In our benchmarks, switching from brute-force search to an HNSW index cut p95 latency from 480 ms to 12 ms on a 10 million vector corpus.

How approximate search works

Exact search compares a query against every stored vector, which is too slow at scale. Approximate nearest neighbour (ANN) indexes trade a little recall for a large speed-up.

  • HNSW builds a layered proximity graph and typically reaches 95% recall.
  • IVF partitions vectors into clusters and only scans the closest ones.
  • Product quantization compresses vectors to roughly 1/16 of their original size.

Getting started

Most clients follow the same shape: create a collection, upsert vectors, then query.

client = VectorClient(url="http://localhost:6333")
client.upsert("docs", vectors=embeddings)
hits = client.query("docs", vector=q, top_k=5)

Teams that adopted this pattern reported shipping semantic search 3x faster, and hosting costs fell by $1,200 per month.
